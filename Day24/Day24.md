## Report Review: Section by Section

### 1. Business Reality Check
- **Who pays?** Near term, a small share of students (often with a parent approving). Real money likely from colleges, where the TPO is the economic buyer.
- **Why pay?** Students want a clear path and proof for recruiters. Colleges want placement percentage, NAAC/NIRF reporting and early at-risk alerts. Nobody will pay for roadmaps; they pay for **verification, accountability and outcomes**.
- **Biggest risks:** "ChatGPT is free" caps willingness to pay, INR 1,500 ARPU is thin, founder GTM is weak, and college sales cycles are slow.

**Weak assumptions to validate first**

| # | Assumption | Pass line |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Students will pay something | 8%+ pay or pre-commit |
| 2 | Product beats a one-prompt LLM answer | Most of 10 students prefer ours |
| 3 | A 7-day visible win drives retention | 40%+ weekly-active at week 4 |
| 4 | TPO is the budget holder and will pay | 3+ commits; budget named |
| 5 | Parents will fund bigger purchases | Parent payer named in 3+ interviews |
| 6 | Pain points (proof of skill, failed OAs) are real | 60%+ rate pain 7+/10 |
| 7 | LLM + support cost is affordable | Under ~15% of ARPU |

**Illustrative Year-1 revenue (assumptions, not forecasts)**

| Stream | Downside | Base | Upside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free users | 3,000 | 10,000 | 25,000 |
| Student Plus | 1.8 L | 7.5 L | 22.5 L |
| Pay-per-use | 0.9 L | 3.0 L | 7.5 L |

**Most plausible path to INR 1 crore ARR:** ~20 colleges (40 L) + ~2,000 payers at INR 3,000 blended (60 L).

### 4. Go-To-Market Strategy
Community-led B2C first (speed, proof, data), then use that proof to open college pilots (revenue).

| Phase | Goal | Gate to next phase |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Discover (Days 1-14) | Pick wedge; get primary evidence | 30+ interviews, 100+ waitlist |
| 2. Prove (Days 15-30) | Show retention and early revenue | 40%+ retention; 8%+ pay or 3+ colleges |
| 3. Community scale (Days 31-90) | Grow organically; get testimonials | Repeatable CAC under target; 500+ users |
| 4. Land colleges (Days 60-180) | First paid college contracts | 3+ paid colleges |

|---|---|

**30-day funnel:** 300+ visits, then 100+ waitlist/check completions, then 25-30 pilot students, then 40%+ four-week retention, then 8%+ pay or pre-commit (or 3+ colleges).

### 8. Reverse SWOT Analysis

| | |
|---|---|
| **Weakness to flip into strength** | Solo builder-heavy founder (add a growth/sales co-founder); zero primary evidence (make interviewing the first public skill); narrow MVP (use as focus) |
| **Threat to counter** | Free LLMs (win on verified, tracked progress); look-alike startups (own college relationships and benchmarks); placement seasonality |
| **Strength that could fail** | Strong secondary data may hide a lack of payers; builder speed may build features nobody pays for; computer-vision strength is not the wedge |
| **Opportunity that may be a mirage** | Large audience does not mean large willingness to pay; TPOs may be vendor-locked; credentials only matter if employers adopt them |

## Key Learnings

- **A real problem is not a business.** Pain and market size are documented, but nobody has paid yet. Payment is the only evidence that counts.
- **Roadmaps are a commodity.** Free LLMs already give them away, so the product must compete on verification, tracking and outcomes.
- **The payer may not be the user.** Colleges (TPOs) and parents are likelier sources of real revenue than students at INR 1,500/year.
- **Distribution is the founder's weak spot.** Low-cost organic channels (WhatsApp, ambassadors, a shareable card) and a growth co-founder matter as much as the build.
- **Sequence matters.** Students first for speed and proof, colleges second for revenue.
