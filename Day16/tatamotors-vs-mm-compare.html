Business Note: JLR
Summary

Tata Motors — Business

India's largest CV maker by volume, and owner of Jaguar Land Rover (JLR), the British luxury brand. Post the October 2025 demerger, the group's listed entities are Tata Motors Ltd (Commercial Vehicles) and Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles Ltd (which includes JLR).

JLR is a capex-heavy, cyclical global luxury business exposed to UK/EU tariffs and China demand, and contributes the majority of the group's consolidated revenue when both entities are combined.

Mahindra & Mahindra — Business

India's largest SUV maker by revenue market share and the country's largest tractor manufacturer by volume. M&M operates across Automotive, Farm Equipment, Financial Services, and Real Estate segments.

FY26 revenue grew ~25% YoY on strong SUV volumes (Scorpio, XUV, Thar, Bolero, EV models) and continued tractor market leadership. [Source: Craytheon/Anand Rathi]

Business Note: The JLR Cyberattack

In September 2025, JLR (under Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles) suffered a major cyberattack that forced a shutdown of production across UK, Slovakia, Brazil and India plants for roughly five weeks. This is one of the costliest cyber incidents in UK corporate history.

The disruption pushed JLR to a pre-tax loss for the following quarters (versus a prior-year profit), with wholesale volumes down over 40% at the worst point, and prompted a £1.5 billion UK government loan guarantee to stabilise the supply chain. Production returned to normal levels by mid-November 2025. [Sources: Manufacturing Digital, Fintool, Express & Star]

This event is central to understanding Tata Motors' consolidated (CV + JLR) earnings volatility this year and should be factored into any FY26 comparison against M&M's steadier growth.

Where Tata Motors Leads

• Larger promoter commitment (42.6% vs 18.1%)
• Iconic global luxury brand (JLR) with long-term recovery upside once cyber-incident effects fade
• India's largest CV franchise by volume

Where M&M Leads

• Meaningfully cheaper on P/E (24.1x vs ≈52x)
• Much higher, more consistent net profit (₹18,470 Cr TTM)
• Steadier ~25% YoY revenue growth with no one-off shock this year
• Pays a dividend; Tata Motors CV currently does not

Neutral Summary

These are two very different bets right now. M&M looks like the steadier, more consistently profitable business on nearly every metric available this session, while Tata Motors' numbers are harder to read cleanly because of the October 2025 demerger and the JLR cyberattack's lingering effect on group earnings. Several ratios (interest coverage, precise D/E, full ownership breakup for Tata Motors) could not be verified from available sources and are flagged rather than estimated. There is no winner declared here — only a picture of where each currently stands.
