DECISION REPORT

How to prepare for a Google SWE placement in the next 6 months

1. The Real Decision

You are choosing how to split six months between DSA and core CS, balanced prep with AI/ML projects and applications, or an AI/ML-first portfolio.

The trade-off is depth in interview fundamentals against breadth in projects and applications.

It hurts because Google is the target and a wrong bet feels like lost time you can't recover.

2. The Case for Each Option

Option A: DSA + Core CS first

Strongest case

Your goal is a Google SWE role, and DSA, problem-solving and CS fundamentals are exactly what you named as needing strength. Your gut already points here.

Hidden upside

Strong fundamentals make every later project and interview easier.

Biggest weakness

A thin resume if projects stall completely.

Best if you value

Interview readiness over a flashy portfolio.

Option B: Balanced prep + AI/ML projects + applications

Strongest case

Covers skills, resume and real applications at once, so progress shows up on several fronts.

Hidden upside

Live applications give you real interview feedback early.

Biggest weakness

Splitting time can leave DSA shallow, which is your stated worry.

Best if you value

Flexibility and keeping other offers open.

Option C: AI/ML portfolio first

Strongest case

Builds a distinctive resume in a field you are already interested in.

Hidden upside

Projects give you real stories for behavioral rounds.

Biggest weakness

Does the least for the DSA and CS fundamentals your Google goal depends on.

Best if you value

Standing out over passing a standard screen.

3. Assumption Buster

Assumptions you may be making

  • That one strategy must be picked and held for six months. You said yourself you can adjust.
  • That "is my level enough?" can be answered by feeling. You have no measured baseline yet.
  • That Google is the only outcome worth planning around. You said other companies are an option.

Biases at play

  • Loss aversion: the fear of wasting six months is weighing more than the gains of any plan.
  • Confirmation bias: your gut already says DSA first, so you may only be looking for evidence that agrees.

What you are ignoring

You haven't measured where you stand today. Without a mock-interview baseline, every option is a guess.

4. Decision Matrix

A DSA + Core CS   B Balanced   C AI/ML Portfolio

Scores reflect your stated goal, your gut lean, and your note that you can adjust and apply elsewhere.

5. Premortem

Option A failed after 12 months

  • You drilled problems without timed mocks, so interview pressure still felt new.
  • Projects and applications were dropped and the resume stayed thin.
  • You kept studying instead of testing readiness.

Early warning sign

Mock scores flat after four weeks.

Prevention action

Book a timed mock every week and change topics based on the result.

Option B failed after 12 months

  • Time was split too thin and nothing reached interview depth.
  • Projects expanded and ate the DSA hours.
  • Applications were sent before you were ready.

Early warning sign

DSA hours slip below plan two weeks in a row.

Prevention action

Fix a weekly time split, with DSA as the largest block and projects capped.

6. 7-Day Test Plan

  • Day 1-2: List your weakest DSA and CS topics and find a mock-interview format to use.
  • Day 3-4: Take one timed mock interview and record your score.
  • Day 5-6: Talk to a senior or peer who prepared for a similar role about their time split.
  • Day 7: Commit to A if the mock exposes DSA gaps. Choose B if the gaps are small.

7. The Verdict

Option A: DSA + Core CS first

It matches your Google goal and your gut, and it directly attacks the fundamentals you named as the gap.

Keep projects and applications as a capped side track, not an equal share.

What could flip it

A baseline mock showing your DSA is already solid would shift weight to Option B.

Six months is enough only if you measure readiness with timed mocks, not with how many hours you studied.
